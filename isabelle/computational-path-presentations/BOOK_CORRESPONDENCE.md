# Book correspondence and extension boundaries

## Source identity

The book is *Computational Paths: The Calculus of Equality*, by Arthur Freitas
Ramos, Ruy J. G. B. de Queiroz, Anjolina Grisi de Oliveira and Dov M. Gabbay,
College Publications, Studies in Logic: Mathematical Logic and Foundations,
volume 117 (2026), ISBN 978-1-84890-515-3.

Publisher: https://www.collegepublications.co.uk/logic/mlf/?00037=

The inspected texts were a 534-page manuscript draft and a distinct 611-page
companion preprint. They are not established as byte-identical to the published
edition. Numbered correspondences below explicitly identify these manuscripts;
no final-print pagination or numbered edition is inferred. Rule counts also vary
between these manuscripts and are not silently transferred. The manuscript PDFs
and lengthy extracts are not part of this source distribution.

The book has four authors. Its bibliography is separate from the prepared
three-author metadata for this formal development; neither source citation nor
metadata preparation certifies individual formalization contributions or consent.

## Mathematical correspondences

### Presented words and independent binary witnesses

Chapter 13 defines generators with endpoint maps, binary path expressions and
the congruence generated by structural laws and parallel declared equations.
Definitions 13.4–13.5 appear on printed page 334 of the 534-page draft and page
362 of the 611-page companion.

The word presentation `cp_equiv` and the independently generated typed tree
congruence formalize this algebraic level. Tree congruence is generated from
explicit groupoid equations and typed context rules, not defined to mean
word equivalence after flattening. Its correspondence with signed words is a
proved adequacy theorem. A further independently generated presented-tree
congruence admits named parallel tree equations and corresponds exactly to the
word presentation obtained by flattening only well-typed declarations. This
guard is essential: flattening can erase an invalid identity vertex. These
are equational results, not termination or confluence of an oriented binary-tree
rewrite system.

### Changes of presentation

The relation-replacement, redundant-relation and reversible-interpretation
results are derived consequences of the presentation framework. They are not
claimed to reproduce numbered Tietze theorems in the book. Chapter 15's
universal-property viewpoint motivates word-valued interpretations: the
relevant statement is Corollary 15.10 on printed page 384 of the older draft,
and Corollary 15.11 on page 417 of the companion.

The formal development does not yet establish initiality into every arbitrary
external groupoid. Its interpretations have explicitly defined typed word
presentations as targets. Preservation alone is not used as evidence for
reflection; the reversible case supplies separate roundtrip hypotheses.

### The algebraic torus presentation

Section 13.3, Proposition 13.15 and Theorem 13.16 describe the two-loop
presentation with the commutation relation AB=BA: printed pages 340–341 in the
older draft and pages 368–369 in the companion.

The formalized result concerns precisely that algebraic one-object presentation.
Its integer exponent pair and signed-power representative provide a complete
normal form for an independently defined, nonempty relation set. Signed
commutations must be derived from AB=BA, not silently added as extra axioms.
The independently declared binary-tree commutation equation is also translated
exactly, yielding sound and complete executable normalization for binary torus
witnesses, not only flat words.
This does not identify the presentation with the fundamental group of a
geometric torus: no realization, continuous path, homotopy or topological
comparison theorem is supplied here.

## Existing formalization reuse and novelty limits

The entry continues to import AFP Free-Groups directly. That entry already
contains cancellation, normalization and a universal property, and its
`Isomorphisms` theory identifies the one-generator free group with integers.
AFP Fundamental_Group_Circle proves the stronger geometric circle result.
A fresh signed-word circle example would not establish new mathematical novelty.

AFP Category3 has free-category infrastructure; Catoids and
CZH_Elementary_Categories already have groupoid interfaces. These were inspected,
not imported merely to suggest reuse. The earlier dependent-HIT project also has
presented quotient hom-sets and word-valued functors. Repeating that quotient
scaffolding alone is not the purpose of these extensions.

Isabelle/HOL already constructs free abelian groups using finitely supported
integer functions in `HOL/Algebra/Free_Abelian_Groups.thy`, including group laws,
basis induction and homomorphism extension. Joseph Thommes and Manuel Eberl's
AFP [Finitely Generated Abelian Groups](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Finitely_Generated_Abelian_Groups.html)
proves invariant-factor and primary decompositions. The rank-two integer target
is existing algebra, not a new general classification result. Those semantic
results do not by themselves prove that the independently generated signed-word
congruence with the sole relation AB=BA has exactly that kernel. The constructive
normalization and its independent binary-tree correspondence supply this bridge.
Neither theory is imported merely to restate existing abstract algebra.

The added value is a reusable presentation-change calculus, a complete decision
result for a particular nonempty presentation, and an independent syntax
adequacy theorem. This is a scoped source-overlap assessment, not a worldwide
novelty claim or an AFP editorial decision.

## Scope retained

No full LND equality rewrite calculus, including the 39-rule system or its
expanded manuscript versions, dependent eliminator, higher
coherence theorem, univalence, or HoTT interpretation is imported or assumed.
All relevant correctness claims rest on the independently stated Isabelle
relations and checked proofs. In particular, neither a structural normalizer
nor a presentation's universal property decides arbitrary added equations.
Future integration with HoTT requires explicit interpretations and their
preservation and, where needed, reflection theorems.
