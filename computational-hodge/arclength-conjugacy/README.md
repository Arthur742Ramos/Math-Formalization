# Arclength conjugacy and geometric resonance

For every positive C1 density on the real scalar period-one circle, the normalized arclength coordinate and its inverse induce a based circle homeomorphism and mutually inverse maps on actual C2 coefficients. The weighted circle Laplacian, with the nonnegative geometer sign, intertwines with the flat Laplacian under this pullback, scaled by the inverse square of the density's mean.

Pulling back the flat cosine gives a nonzero eigenmode for every such density, with eigenvalue `m⁻²(2π)²` for mean density `m > 0`. Its weighted raw integrals and finite harmonic-cochain periods vanish on every actual based computational trace, yet the mode itself is not in the range of the Laplacian shifted by that eigenvalue, even when all C2 competitors are allowed. An explicit positive nonconstant density has a nonidentity real arclength coordinate.

The result uses the actual endpoint-indexed binary trace syntax, realization, covering lift and ordinary integral through the trace's corners. The mapped phase loop has covering winding `n * count p` for every natural `n`; the first eigenmode evaluated on the actual trace is the cosine of the lifted phase at `n = 1`. The all-`n` statement concerns phase-loop winding, not an all-frequency eigenmode theorem.

These are classical one-dimensional results formalized for this computational-path model. The package does not claim a complete spectrum, eigenbasis or exact multiplicity, an intrinsic general-manifold result, an analytic Kähler or de Rham Hodge theorem, arbitrary open-path periods, or mathematical novelty. Classical differential-geometric context is in Danny Calegari, [*Introduction to Differential Geometry*](https://math.uchicago.edu/~dannyc/courses/riem_geo_2019/differential_geometry.pdf), sections 5.1 and 9.2.
